The businesses getting real leverage from AI and the businesses producing forgettable content are using the same tools. The difference isn't the model. It's where in the process they put it.

Here's the framework I run with clients.

## Rule one: AI drafts, it doesn't decide

Strategy, positioning, and point of view are the parts of your marketing that competitors can't copy. They're also the parts a general-purpose model is worst at, because it's optimised toward the average of everything it has read.

Give the model the judgement calls and you get output that reads exactly like your competitor's output — because it is.

So split the work:

- **You decide:** what to say, who it's for, what you believe that others don't, what claim you're willing to stand behind.
- **AI executes:** first drafts, variations, summarisation, reformatting, translation, structure.

Direction stays human. Volume becomes cheap.

## Rule two: feed it your material, not the internet's

A model asked to "write a post about SEO" returns the consensus of the internet. A model given your last client call transcript, your actual results, and your own writing returns something only you could have published.

Build a reference file and reuse it in every prompt:

- How you actually talk — paste three things you wrote yourself.
- Your real numbers and case studies.
- The objections you hear weekly.
- Terms you never use because they sound like everyone else.

This single habit is the difference between AI as a copy machine and AI as an amplifier.

## Rule three: automate the work with no voice in it

The highest-return uses of AI in most businesses aren't content at all. They're the invisible operational work:

1. **Lead triage.** Score and route incoming enquiries so hot leads reach a human in minutes.
2. **First-response drafts.** Not sent automatically — drafted, then approved.
3. **Reporting.** Pull the numbers, write the summary, flag what moved. This alone gave one team back most of a working week each month.
4. **Repurposing.** One long piece into the email, the carousel, the script.
5. **Research synthesis.** Reading competitors, reviews, and search data faster than a person can.

Nobody's brand was ever damaged by an automated reporting pipeline.

> Use AI where the work is repetitive and the voice is irrelevant. Guard the places where the voice is the product.

## Rule four: keep a human checkpoint on anything published

Every AI workflow that touches a customer needs one named person who approves before it ships. Not a policy — a person.

Models still invent statistics, still soften claims into meaninglessness, still produce a paragraph that is confidently wrong about your own pricing. The cost of one published error is far higher than the minutes review takes.

## Rule five: measure it like anything else

If a workflow doesn't reduce hours, increase output, or improve a conversion rate, it's a hobby. Track the before and after. Kill what doesn't earn its place.

## The honest summary

AI won't give you a strategy, a point of view, or a reason for anyone to choose you. It removes the friction between having those things and executing them at volume.

Which means it widens the gap. Businesses that know what they stand for get dramatically faster. Businesses that don't get dramatically more content that sounds like everyone else's.
