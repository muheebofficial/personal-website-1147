# **Q4 Is Coming: Why Your Google Product Feed Could Make or Break Your Ecommerce Sales**

You can have the right products, the right prices, the right offers and the right advertising budget—and still lose sales if Google doesn't properly understand your products.

Q4 is approaching.

For ecommerce businesses, that means Black Friday, Cyber Monday, Christmas shopping, end-of-year promotions, increased search demand and, inevitably, more competition for the same customers.

Everyone wants to sell more during Q4. Businesses increase their Google Ads budgets, they launch promotions, they redesign landing pages, they create new ad creatives and offer discounts.

But there's one part of the ecommerce advertising machine that many businesses don't think about until something goes wrong:

Their Google product feed.

And this is where things get interesting.

Because your Google product feed isn't just a technical file sitting somewhere inside Merchant Center.

It is one of the ways Google understands what you're selling.

If your product information is incomplete, inaccurate, poorly structured or inconsistent with your website, you could be making it harder for Google to show your products to the right people at the right time.

And during Q4, when competition is at its highest, that's a problem you don't want to discover after you've already increased your advertising budget.

Your Google Ads Campaign Isn't the Whole Story
One of the biggest misconceptions I see in ecommerce marketing is that performance starts with the advertising campaign.

It doesn't. It starts much earlier. Before someone clicks your Shopping ad, Google needs to understand things such as:

•	What the product is
•	What it costs
•	Whether it's available
•	What brand it belongs to
•	What category it belongs to
•	What it looks like
•	Which searches it is relevant to
•	Where it can be delivered
•	Whether there are promotions
•	What other products are related to it

That's where your product data becomes incredibly important. Think about it this way.

Your Google Ads account is the salesperson. Your product feed is part of the salesperson's product catalogue. If the catalogue contains incomplete or incorrect information, the salesperson is working with bad information. And no amount of shouting "SELL MORE!" at the salesperson fixes a broken catalogue.

## ****What Happens When Your Product Feed Isn't Ready for Q4?**
Imagine you own an ecommerce store selling fashion, electronics, beauty products or home appliances. You decide to spend ₦5 million—or $5,000, $20,000 or more—on Q4 advertising. You have great products, your offers are competitive, your website looks good, your campaigns are launched.

But then you discover: Some products are disapproved, some prices don't match your website, some products are showing as unavailable, your bestsellers don't have the right product identifiers, your product images aren't competitive, your promotional pricing isn't properly communicated, your landing pages don't match what the feed is telling Google.

Now you're paying for traffic while parts of your product catalogue aren't working as effectively as they should.

>That's why I don't look at ecommerce advertising as simply an "ads problem.

It is a system problem.
And your product feed is one of the systems that needs to be right.

Things to look out for 

1. Your Product Titles Can Influence How Google Understands Your Products
Let's start with something deceptively simple:
Product titles: “Compare Nike Shoes” with “Nike Air Max Men's Running Shoes, Black/White, Size 42”
The second title provides substantially more context.
Your product titles should help both Google and shoppers understand exactly what the product is.

Depending on the product, useful information can include:
•	Brand
•	Product type
•	Model
•	Gender
•	Colour
•	Size
•	Material
•	Key specification
•	Relevant distinguishing feature

This doesn't mean stuffing keywords into your titles. It means communicating product information clearly…And that distinction matters.

Because your goal isn't to write titles for an algorithm.
Your goal is to make it easier for Google to understand the product and easier for the shopper to decide whether the product is relevant.

2. Your Product Images Need to Compete for Attention
Q4 is noisy. Your customer is going to see products from multiple businesses competing for attention. If your product image looks poor beside your competitors, you have a problem before the customer ever visits your website.

This is why I recommend thinking beyond: "Do we have a product image?" Instead, ask: "Does our product imagery help us compete?" “Does it help us convert”
Consider using:

•	High-quality product photography
•	Multiple product angles
•	Close-up images
•	Lifestyle imagery
•	Product-in-use images
•	Variant images
•	Product videos where appropriate

The purpose isn't simply to make your Merchant Center account look nice. It's to help the customer understand what they're buying. And in ecommerce, better understanding can reduce hesitation.

3. Price and Availability Must Tell the Same Story
This is one of the easiest ways to create friction.
Imagine someone sees ₦85,000 on Google, they click. But our website says ₦99,000.

That's not a small technical problem. That's a trust problem.

The same applies to availability. If Google says In stock but your website says out of stock, you've created a poor experience.

Before Q4, make sure your feed and website agree on:
•	Price
•	Sale price
•	Availability
•	Product variants
•	Product landing pages
Your advertising system should not be telling customers one thing while your website tells them another.

4. Fix Merchant Center Disapprovals Before You Increase Your Budget
This should be one of your first Q4 checks.
Open Google Merchant Center, look at your product diagnostics, and find out:
What is disapproved?
Then ask:
Why?
And more importantly, Which of my important products are affected? Don't treat every product equally.

If 15 low-priority products have issues, that's different from your top 15 bestsellers being unavailable to Shopping campaigns. Prioritize the products that matter most to your revenue.

5. Your Product Identifiers Matter
GTINs, brand information and other product identifiers might not be the most exciting part of ecommerce marketing. But they matter. Accurate identifiers can help Google better understand and match products.

If you have hundreds or thousands of SKUs, manually assuming everything is correct is a risky strategy. Audit them.

Especially for:
•	Bestselling products
•	Branded products
•	High-value products
•	Products you're heavily promoting during Q4

Technical accuracy isn't glamorous. But it can be expensive when you ignore it.

6. Prepare Your Q4 Promotions Before the Rush
Black Friday shouldn't be the day you start thinking about Black Friday.

If you're planning: 20% off, Buy 2, Get 1 Free, Free Shipping, Christmas Bundles, Black Friday Pricing, Limited-Time Discounts, then your product feed, Merchant Center setup, website and advertising campaigns should all be prepared to communicate those offers properly.

Think about your promotional strategy before launch day. Which products are going on sale? Which products are excluded? When does the promotion begin? When does it end? Are sale prices correctly reflected? Does the landing page show the same offer? Does the ad communicate the same offer?

Consistency is critical.

7. Use Custom Labels to Tell Google Which Products Matter
If you have a large catalogue, don't treat every SKU as though it has exactly the same commercial value.
Your Q4 strategy might include labels such as:

•	Bestseller
•	High Margin
•	Black Friday
•	Christmas
•	New Product
•	Clearance
•	Seasonal
•	High Priority
•	Fast Moving
•	Low Stock

This gives you another layer of organisation for your campaign strategy.

For example, imagine you discover that your bestsellers + high-margin products are responsible for a significant portion of your revenue. You don't want your Q4 strategy blindly distributing attention across thousands of products. You want to know where the commercial opportunity is.

Your product feed can help you structure that thinking.

8. Don't Forget Shipping and Returns
Here's a question every ecommerce business should be asking before Q4:
>What happens after someone clicks Buy?" Especially during the Christmas period.

Customers want to know:
•	How much is delivery?
•	How long will it take?
•	Where do you deliver?
•	Can I return the product?
•	How long do I have to return it?
•	What happens if the product arrives damaged?

These aren't just customer-service questions, they're conversion questions, when customers have uncertainty, they hesitate. And when they hesitate, your beautifully optimised advertising campaign can still fail to produce the sale.

9. Your Website Still Has to Convert
This is where I believe many ecommerce businesses get the whole equation wrong.
They obsess over getting traffic. But traffic isn't revenue.

Revenue happens when traffic converts. You can have an excellent Google Ads, a healthy product feed, a strong search visibility, great product imagery and still have disappointing sales if your website is difficult to use. That's why I look at ecommerce marketing from a broader perspective.

Your Product feed → Ads → Landing page → Product page → Checkout → Conversion
should work as one system.
If one part breaks, the whole customer journey suffers.

10. Q4 Is Also an SEO and Search Visibility Opportunity
There's another reason I wouldn't treat your product feed as an isolated Google Ads issue. People don't only discover products through traditional Shopping ads anymore. Search is becoming increasingly conversational, visual and AI-driven. Customers can ask much more specific questions about what they want, they can compare products, they can search by images, they can discover products through different Google surfaces.

And AI-powered search experiences are changing how people move from "I need something" to "I want this specific product."

That means ecommerce businesses need to think beyond traditional SEO.

You need to think about: SEO, AEO, AIO, Product data, Structured information, Content, Conversion optimisation.
The businesses that provide richer, clearer information give search and AI systems more context to work with.

So, Is Your Ecommerce Business Actually Ready for Q4?
Here's the question I want you to ask yourself:
If I doubled my Google Ads budget tomorrow, would my ecommerce infrastructure be ready to handle it?

Not:"Can I spend more?"
But: "Can my system convert more?"

Check your:
**Product Feed**
•	Are your titles accurate?
•	Are your descriptions useful?
•	Are your images competitive?
•	Are your prices correct?
•	Is availability accurate?
•	Are your identifiers properly configured?

**Merchant Center**
•	Are important products approved?
•	Are there unresolved warnings?
•	Are there disapprovals?
•	Are promotions configured correctly?

**Website**
•	Do your product pages match your ads?
•	Is your website fast?
•	Is the checkout process simple?
•	Does your mobile experience work properly?
•	Are your offers clearly communicated?

**Conversion**
•	Are visitors turning into customers?
•	Where are people dropping off?
•	Are there unnecessary checkout barriers?
•	Are your product pages answering customer objections?

**Marketing**
•	Which products deserve more budget?
•	Which products have the highest margins?
•	Which products are seasonal?
•	Which products should you push during Black Friday?
•	Which audiences should you retarget?

That's the real Q4 preparation.
